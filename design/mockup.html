From the file manager

Select images or folders in Finder, Explorer, Dolphin or Nautilus, right-click, and pick a pinned recipe. A small unit opens, runs the batch and reports what it saved, with undo one press away. "Open in Wrangler…" loads the same files into the main window instead.

Why it is laid out like this

The display shows the whole plan before anything is written. The sheet shows every photo as it will come out, with its new name and size; the monitor shows one photo before and after, with the trim box in red and the canvas edge in orange (the same colours as their modules).

The rack is the recipe, top to bottom in the order it runs. One colour per kind of step, on its tag and knob caps. The lit key turns a step off without losing its settings. Drag the grip to reorder; the empty slot adds a step.

Plain words first, numbers underneath. "Look: Balanced" aims at a measured visual score, so it means the same thing for every photo and format. The exact encoder setting is still shown in the monitor for anyone who wants it.

Attention comes to you. The tabs count files that need a decision (kept original because the new one was bigger, name clashes, failures) and the sheet sorts them first, so a 1,284-file batch can be checked in seconds.

The meter is the receipt. IN and OUT ladders and the dot-matrix saving are estimates before the run, count live during it, and stay as the receipt afterwards, next to Undo.

Everything works from the keyboard. The hint strip lists the keys for what is on screen; ⌘K finds any action. Knobs take arrows, Shift for fine steps, and a double-click to type an exact value.
